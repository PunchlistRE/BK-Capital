History
The Fife ’n Drum Restaurant & Inn is more than just an eatery  — it’s a Kent institution, deeply woven into the fabric of the town’s identi-
ty. Located at 53 Main Street, the Fife ’n Drum first opened its doors on January 20, 1973, when pianist and musician Dolph Traymon and his wife Audrey chose Kent
as the place to build a legacy. Traymon, an accomplished pianist, envisioned a restaurant where fine food and live music were inseparable. From the beginning, he
sat down at his Steinway and made music a central part of the dinner experience. In its early years, the Fife faced all the challenges typical of a new enterprise in a
small town. The 1970s energy crisis, logistical constraints, and the lean seasons all tested the Traymons’ resolve. Yet, with persistence and a commitment to quality
— their produce, meat, and baked goods often logged long distances to reach the kitchen — they began to build a devoted clientele. Over time, the Fife ’n Drum
grew in both depth and reputation, adding expansions, a well-curated wine cellar, a gift shop, and even an inn.
When Dolph passed away on Christmas Eve 2016, he had remained active — still playing piano nearly until the end. His daughter, Elissa Potts, had long been pre-
paring to carry the torch; she officially joined the management ranks in the late 1970s and later partnered with her husband, George Potts, to sustain and deepen
the family tradition. Under their stewardship, the Fife has continued to evolve its culinary offerings — blending time-honored signature dishes (like Duck Flambé,
Filet au Poivre, and Caesar Salad for Two) with modern sensibilities and adaptations to dietary preferences.
Music remains central. The “Fridays @ the Fife” live‐music series, once driven by Dolph’s piano, now features a broader roster of performers — including George,
local jazz and folk artists, and guests who carry on the spirit of live performance.
Over five decades, the restaurant has accommodated multiple generations of patrons: children of earlier diners now dine with their own children at the Fife. In
2023, the Fife ’n Drum celebrated its 50th anniversary, with commemorative menus, music events, and tributes to its enduring presence in Kent.
In short, the Fife ’n Drum is not just a restaurant — it is a musical, cultural, and social landmark. Its evolution mirrors that of Kent itself, rising from modest roots to
becoming a destination for fine dining, intimate hospitality, and memorable experiences. The Fife ‘n Drum legacy illustrates how a small building — when guided
by passion, continuity, and community roots — can transcend ordinary expectations to become a timeless anchor in a town’s identity.
