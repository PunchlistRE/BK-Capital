Run everything: `PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE=<path> CHROMIUM_PATH=<chrome> audit/run_all.sh` (CI runs it on every push).

## Reproducing a result
Every deal is a complete input set in `deals/index.js` (or `real/real_deals.json`). `bkCreateNewDeal(deal)` then the engine
functions give the output; `suite_run.js` shows exactly which. Nothing depends on the clock, the time zone or run order.

## Golden results
`golden/suite_golden.json` holds the key figures of every deal. A change in the engine that moves any of them fails the
suite. If the change is intended (a fix or a deliberate methodology change), review the diff, then
`python3 audit/suite_test.py <suite.json> --update-golden` and commit the new file with the change that caused it.

## Real deals
`real/build_real_deals.py <workbook.xlsm> real/real_deals.json` builds app inputs from the Yardi T-12 **line items** (the source of truth):
**NOI = income lines - operating expense lines; price = NOI / the Property Summary's market cap rate.** Debt service and owner
distributions are not operating expenses. The Property Summary's own NOI is not used (it differs from the line items by 30-60%).

The workbook is OCR of scanned Yardi statements, so each section's lines are checked against that section's printed total. A line
is corrected (months sum, moved decimal point, flipped sign) **only if that closes the section's gap**; otherwise the lines are kept
and the mismatch is flagged. The result is then tied to the statement's own NET INCOME (+ mortgage interest): all six residential
properties agree within 0.1%. Items Yardi reports that are non-recurring or non-operating (bank interest, settlements, insurance
claims, partnership tax filings) are left in as reported and listed in each deal's `flags` for the underwriter to decide.
Financing, growth and JV terms are the app's standard assumptions (the workbook has none). Rent is collected rent, so vacancy and bad debt are zero.

## Cost segregation / tax-grade depreciation
Per deal, Inputs > Depreciation. New deals are stamped `depMethod: 'macrs'`; a saved deal without it keeps the simplified
straight-line schedule until someone switches it (banner + button). `real/harvard_assets.json` is the itemised 169-asset study
from `Cost_Segregation_Harvard.xlsx`; the HARVARD deal loads it with `bkDeprSeedHarvard()` and ties to the workbook (bonus
$1,207,058.89, tax savings $446,611.79 at 37%, 89.3:1 payback, $108,010.91 a year on the 39-year class). One deliberate
difference: the workbook shows no 39-year depreciation in 2025; the IRS mid-month rule gives half a month ($4,500) for a
December 11 in-service date, and the engine follows the IRS.

## Credit & IC (from the Capital Markets model)
Rail group **Credit & IC**: `Credit Policy` (per-deal house limits, gate weights and severities; CMM defaults), `Debt Capacity`
(largest supportable senior loan under LTV, LTC, DSCR at the coupon and at a stressed coupon, interest-only DSCR and debt yield,
the binding constraint, headroom, overage vs the loan in force, leverage ladder, highest coupon holding the minimum DSCR) and
`IC Memo` (policy test table, weighted score, hard/soft exceptions, verdict, break-even occupancy / NOI tolerance / break-even rent /
exit-cap cushion / upside-downside IRR, risk register, conditions precedent, credit override that needs YES + reason + approver).
It tests the deal the engines produced and never changes a return or a loan. Development deals are gated on the tests that apply
to them. Break-evens are bisections against the live engine (`bkRunWith`). Not ported: lender eligibility, capital-stack
structures, refinance timing, fund / portfolio impact (the app has no equivalent engine to hang them on yet).

## Conflicting-input checks (Model Checks)
`bkConflicts()` runs ~45 cross-field rules (tax / depreciation, statement-vs-cash-flow reconciliation, debt, waterfall, development,
credit policy). Each returns a plain-English message with the deal's own numbers and a *why it matters* note on the accounting, tax
or credit consequence. ERROR (inputs contradict, results wrong) feeds `bkModelControls` as FAIL and blocks decision-grade; WARN is
listed; INFO states an assumption. WARN / INFO can be acknowledged per deal. When an edit CREATES a new ERROR / WARN a card
explains it at once (off under automation unless `window.BK_CONFLICT_TOASTS === true`). `conflicts_test.js`: every rule has a
scenario that fires it, clears it when fixed, and checks the explanation; plus readiness integration, acknowledgement and the
pop-up lifecycle. `suite_test.py` checks the reconciliation gap against the independent model and pins each deal's rule IDs.

## Refi / Recap (from the Capital Markets model)
`Debt > Refi / Recap`: for the chosen year and for every possible year, the new loan (lowest of LTV, DSCR, debt yield), net cash-out
after costs and prepayment, post-refinance coverage, and the equity cash flow rebuilt monthly with the new debt service and payoff
(IRR, multiple, profit, cash returned before exit vs. holding the original loan), a verdict, and a reconciliation (closed-form existing
balance, profit bridge). `refi_ref.py` re-derives all of it from `model.py`'s monthly cash flow; REF-1 uses custom inputs.

## Input flow (`input_flow_test.js`)
Leaving a cell re-renders the sheet so the value commits and derived rows refresh. A final `showSheet` wrapper keeps the page's scroll
position, hands focus back to the cell you were heading for (including dropdowns), and coalesces a re-render requested from inside a
blur (which used to throw). Arrow Down / Up commit the current input and move to the next / previous one; entering a new section
brings it to the top of the view and focuses its first input. Selects keep their own arrow keys; Enter and Escape are unchanged.

## Operating Business (from the Punchlist model)
- `biz_ref.py` is an independent implementation of Punchlist_Business_Model_with_Valuation.xlsx (revenue build, P&L, break-even, build-up discount rate, three-case DCF, sensitivities, 10-year valuation). Run alone it ties to the workbook's own outputs (`real/punchlist_inputs.json`, `real/punchlist_expected.json`).
- It also implements the OpCo / PropCo comparison (`place()`): business value with market rent normalised, property value from the independent deal model's Year-1 NOI, and the four sale choices after tax.
- Deals `BIZ-1` (workbook discounting) and `BIZ-2` (bear case, exit multiple discounted at end of year 5, appraised property) are in the deal book and are compared with the app figure by figure; golden results under `:biz`.
- `conflicts_test.js` has a scenario for each `biz.*` rule.

## Import Deal (`import_test.js`)
The Import Deal sheet extracts a Yardi T-12 from a workbook, CSV or a PDF print of one, foots each section to the statement's printed total, and builds a multifamily deal (NOI from the line items; price = NOI / cap rate). The test compares all six residential properties in the Yardi workbook with the independent build (`real/build_real_deals.py` -> `real/real_deals.json`): NOI, price, every expense group, per-unit income and the number of extraction notes; it also runs the ShorePointe PDF through the same extractor and checks it reaches the same NOI. The source files are the user's own data and are not in the repo: set `BK_YARDI_XLSM` and `BK_SHOREPOINTE_PDF` to run those checks, otherwise only the synthetic CSV checks run.
PDF reading is limited to PDFs whose text is stored as ordinary text; a scanned PDF, or one whose fonts use a custom encoding, is refused with a message rather than guessed at.

## Probability-Weighted Returns (`risk_run.js`, `risk_ref.py`)
Two views from the live engine, each on scratch copies of the deal: a scenario-weighted view (Downside / Base / Upside with your probabilities) and a seeded Monte Carlo over exit cap, rent growth, rent level, vacancy, opex level, opex growth and the debt coupon (rent growth correlated with exit cap; draws limited to +/-2.5 standard deviations). `risk_ref.py` re-implements the random generator and driver shifts from the documented algorithm, re-runs draws and all three scenarios through the independent deal model (`model.py`), and recomputes every statistic (mean, standard deviation, percentiles, probabilities, expected profit, the IRR of the probability-weighted cash flow) from the draws' own results. Two deals with non-default probabilities, standard deviations, correlation, seed and draw count: 5,515 checks.
## Import Rent Roll (`import_rr_test.js`)
Reads a unit-level rent roll from CSV, XLSX or a PDF with a text layer, proposes the column mapping, and applies it to the deal's Rent Roll (replace or add). The fixtures are synthetic (no tenant data): `fixtures/make_rent_roll.py` writes a Yardi-style rent roll as CSV and XLSX plus `rent_roll_expected.json`, the truth every unit and total must match; the PDF is printed from the same data at test time. The test checks every field of every unit for all three formats, the summary figures, that the file's own Total row ties, that the operating statement's gross potential rent equals imported in-place rent x 12, and the replace / add behaviour.
The PDF reader decodes embedded fonts through their ToUnicode maps and uses the fonts' own glyph widths to tell one cell from the next; columns are assigned by x-position under the header, so a blank cell does not shift a row.

## Create PDF summary (`bk-v29270-report`)
Home has a **Create PDF summary** button for the active acquisition deal. It opens a seven-page Letter preview (cover, dashboard, financial summary, annual cash flow, distribution waterfall, performance and sensitivity, credit and market) and **Save as PDF** prints it. Everything is read from the live model: `bkProFormaKit.build()`, `bkDealGate()`, `bkRiskScenarios()`, `bkMarketCompare()`; the sensitivity grid reruns `bkMonthlyReturns()` under `bkRunWith`. `ui_test.js` section 16 checks the pages render, the IRR and multiple match the model, and sources equal uses. The financial summary adds the loan terms, the exit (forward NOI, gross sale, costs, payoff, net proceeds), the returns and, when the deal has a rent roll, a summary by unit type and commercial tenant. The annual cash flow runs from a Close column (equity invested) through each year: revenue by component, every expense line, NOI, reserves, interest and principal from the loan schedule, fees, capital events and net cash flow to equity, then coverage, debt yield, cash-on-cash, expense ratio, NOI growth and loan balance; holds over seven years are shown in thousands, holds of up to seven years add a Total column, and holds over ten years continue the annual cash flow and the waterfall on a second page (first half of the years, then the rest); `summary_test.js` renders 2-, 4- and 15-year holds and checks the page count and numbering and that nothing is clipped or overflows. `summary_test.js` checks that every year adds up to the engine's net cash flow, that interest and principal come from the loan schedule, that cash-on-cash is cash flow after debt service over equity, and that nothing on any page runs into the footer. The distribution waterfall page shows the equity split, the promote structure, LP and GP results, and by year each tier's hurdle account (beginning balance, return accrued, contributions, distributions applied, ending balance) with what it paid the LP and GP, the catch-up, and the partner cash flows; the accounts are rebuilt with the engine's own monthly rule, and `summary_test.js` checks that each ends where the engine's does, that every account rolls forward, and that tier distributions add to the LP and GP totals and the promote. The construction funding page rolls the funding engine's monthly schedule up by quarter (costs, equity, loan draws, capitalised interest, loan balance, loan to cost) with a cost-to-date curve; the test checks that sources equal costs each quarter and that the loan rolls forward. Development deals get their own seven pages (cover, dashboard, budget and funding with timeline and takeout, construction funding by quarter, annual cash flow with sensitivity, distribution waterfall, credit decision with delay and exit stress tests); `ui_test.js` section 17 checks them.

## Deal Documents and Import OM (`bk-v29300-docs`, `bk-v29310-om`)
Every deal has **Inputs > Deal Documents**: a property photo (shown on the Overview and on the PDF summary cover and page headers) and slots for the offering memorandum, T-12, CoStar report, listing summary and other files. Files are stored in the browser (IndexedDB) under the deal id, not in the deal's saved inputs. **Inputs > Import OM** reads an OM PDF: a text layer if there is one, otherwise the page images (one JPEG per page) are read with Tesseract.js, loaded from jsDelivr on first use. The contents page is used to find the pages that matter. It parses price, units, area, the unit mix and the income and expense table, builds an acquisition deal from the in-place figures or the broker pro forma, and ties the engine's NOI to the OM's. Creating the deal attaches the OM and the cover picture. Stored OMs and T-12s can also be applied to the current deal from Deal Documents (a version is saved first).
Some OMs (KSR and other small-building brokers, often built in PowerPoint) print a unit-by-unit rent roll and an expense list instead of an income and expense statement, and a two-building offering prints one of each per building with a combined NOI. The importer reads that layout too: every unit (residential or commercial, vacant at its asking rent as the OM counts it), the parking, storage, CAM, laundry and tax pass-through lines as other income, every expense line including three-column expense pages, and the OM's total NOI; a price typed "$12.999,000" is read as $12,999,000 and flagged. The deal is built from the unit rent roll with no vacancy, so its NOI ties to the OM's. If the text recognition engine cannot load, the text pages of a mixed OM are kept rather than abandoned. `om_rentroll_test.js` runs both layouts on synthetic page texts. Income and expense tables are also read when their section headings are letter-spaced ("I N C O M E S U M M A R Y", only at the start of a line) and when they carry Trailing 12, Current and Pro Forma columns: the period columns are read from the column heading, rows that print fewer figures than columns are assigned to the last periods and recorded, the deal is built from the period the user selects (a period the table lacks is an error, never a substitute), and each period's totals are reconciled to the page (expense lines vs Total Expense, EGI less expenses vs NOI, rent plus other income vs EGI). A table with separate commercial and residential rent builds is mapped to the engine's own commercial inputs (the OM's rent and allowance, no recoveries) once the user states the commercial area; the OM's own area statements are listed and never chosen. When the other-income lines disagree with the printed Effective Gross Income, the printed figures and the lines are shown as printed with the combinations of lines that would fit, the build waits for the user's decision (printed totals, every line, or chosen lines), and the printed figures and the decision are saved on the deal (`_omReported`, `_omDecisions`). `om_columns_test.js` covers all of this on page text from three real OMs (`fixtures/om_ie_pages`) and compares existing layouts with the previous build. Price is read separately from every other dollar figure: each price-like figure in the OM is collected with its kind (asking, sold, assessed value, loan), its page and how it was found (label and figure on one line, figure on the line after its label, or a spaced-letter column heading). Only an unambiguous asking price becomes the deal's price; a sold price or an assessed value is listed for the user and never taken, two different asking prices leave the price unset to choose between, and an OM that states no price is flagged "states no price" and cannot be built until one is typed. The OM's printed cap rate (in-place and pro forma) is kept apart and only checks the price against NOI divided by price; the exit cap starts at the calculated rate. `om_price_test.js` covers this on page text from the real OMs (`fixtures/om_price_pages`) and synthetic cases, and compares existing layouts with the previous build. `fixtures/make_om.py` builds a synthetic image-only OM (`sample_om.pdf`, expected values in `sample_om_expected.json`). `ui_test.js` section 20 runs it through the whole path; it needs the recognition engine locally: `npm i tesseract.js@5.1.1 @tesseract.js-data/eng@1.0.0 pdfjs-dist@3.11.174` in a folder and point `BK_TESSERACT_DIR` at its `node_modules/` (the test skips the OM steps if it is missing).

## Tie-out to the owner's master workbook
`gold_tieout.js` loads the ten deals stored in `CRE_MASTER_V7_2_GOLD.xlsm` (SYS_DealDB, extracted to `real/gold_v72.json` with the workbook's stored snapshot results and global assumptions) into this engine and compares each with the workbook: Y1 NOI, loan, equity, unlevered and levered IRR and multiple. `--check` fails if any acquisition deal drifts outside tolerance. It runs the workbook's own conventions: legacy conventions (`BK_CV_LEGACY`), plus the optional settings `lenderNoiBasis=after`, `rentGrowthY1`, `exitNoiBasis=before`, the retail operating expense carried as `miscPerGSF`, and blank taxes estimated ad valorem. ## Property Editor
The Property Editor is seven sheets (Property, Market, Revenues, Expenses, Tenants, Investment, Valuation) with 29 sub-tabs, laid out like an ARGUS Enterprise property: one job per tab, line grids with grouped headers, a hint bar. `editor_test.js` renders every sub-tab for an acquisition and a development deal and checks that each control writes the deal field the rest of the app reads (and that the summaries are the engine's own numbers). `editor_engine_test.js` covers what the editor added to the engine: year-by-year growth curves (a curve that repeats the single rates changes nothing), miscellaneous income lines, and dated capital items (one month's cash flow moves by exactly the amount; NOI never moves). All of it is off by default, so existing deals do not change.

The Reports group has five sheets: Cash Flow Report (annual or per-unit, reconciles to `bkAnnualCF` and the monthly returns, CSV download), Valuation Report (pricing, exit, returns, present value at a chosen rate), Tenant Report (expirations by year and tenants by expiry) and Audit Report (every input as entered with its source and import flag, plus recent changes). They print cleanly. `reports_test.js` checks them against the engine.

The commercial lease model (Tenants > Commercial Leases) replaces the flat retail line with a tenant schedule: each lease has its in-place rent, annual bumps or dated rent steps, expiry and recovery type (NNN pro rata, gross, base year, fixed per SF). At expiry the space renews with the renewal probability or goes dark for the downtime and re-leases at market with free rent; both outcomes are carried as weighted branches and roll again through the hold and the 12 forward months used for the sale price. Tenant improvements and commissions are capital below NOI. In-place rent can also rise by the greater of a floor and the CPI change (measured over the 12 months to a set number of months before each anniversary, with an optional ceiling; known index values are entered on Market Leasing and the CPI forecast carries them forward). Recoveries can be an expense stop, carry a cumulative cap on controllable expenses (not taxes, insurance or utilities), use the shares stated in the lease line by line instead of the area share (a tenant that pays only water takes a share of Utilities), and gross up variable expenses to a set occupancy (the default of 100% bills full-occupancy costs, as before). `lease_terms_test.js` works each of these out independently. `lease_test.js` checks the arithmetic month by month, that a schedule restating the flat inputs reproduces the old NOI and IRR exactly, the editor, and that the Lease Schedule and Cash Flow reports reconcile to the engine.

## Financing Compare (`src/164-bk-fincompare.js`, `fincompare_test.js`)

The Debt group's **Financing Compare** sheet compares up to six loan alternatives on the active acquisition deal by their complete, dated economics, not their stated rate. Each alternative is a set of loan-term overrides (loan-to-value, DSCR and debt-yield sizing, amortisation, interest-only period, fixed or floating rate with index, spread, floor, cap and the deal's index path, points, rate-cap cost, loan term, refinance at maturity and its terms). The engine is run once per alternative on a scratch copy (`bkRunWith`); the sheet reads the engine's monthly loan schedule and levered cash flows and adds no sizing, debt-service or return calculation of its own.

From the schedule it builds the dated financing cash flows (proceeds, fees, interest, principal, refinancing, balloon or sale payoff) and discounts them at one disclosed annual rate on the engine's own day-count (actual days / 365, month-end dates). The default rate is the credit policy's target IRR until the user sets one, and a ±2 point sensitivity shows whether the preferred alternative depends on it. Per alternative it shows: proceeds and required equity, upfront costs, year-1 debt service, base and stressed DSCR (NOI decline, and for a floating loan the index shock run through the engine), debt yield, interest, fees and penalties (undiscounted and PV), net PV of the financing flows, the all-in cost of the loan (IRR of its flows), the balance repaid at sale, maturity and refinance events, and the engine's levered IRR, multiple, profit and cash-on-cash. Winners are listed per measure (lowest PV cost, highest net PV, highest IRR, highest stressed DSCR, least equity); there is no composite score.

Two items the engine does not carry are entered per alternative and charged by the sheet, labelled as such: other upfront costs and a prepayment penalty on a balance repaid before maturity (the refinance loan's own prepayment terms are not an engine input, and defeasance and yield maintenance are not modelled). The sheet shows the engine IRR beside the IRR including those charges. Tax effects are not modelled.

**Reconciliation:** for every alternative and every month, the engine's levered cash flow must equal its unlevered cash flow plus the financing cash flows built from the schedule (the sheet's own two charges excluded); the sheet shows Yes/No and warns when it does not. **Test:** `fincompare_test.js` checks a fixed-rate alternative against closed-form loan mathematics (balance, interest, PV of interest and fees, net PV, all-in cost, stressed DSCR), reconciles five structures (fixed, floating with a cap, refinanced at maturity, interest-only with higher points and a prepayment penalty, balloon with no refinance), confirms the deal is not modified, and exercises edit, add, remove and reset.

## Pre-Offer Bid (`src/165-bk-prebid.js`, `prebid_test.js`)

The Returns & Equity group's **Pre-Offer Bid** sheet finds the maximum purchase price the active acquisition supports under the user's constraints. Constraints (each switchable, targets starting from the credit policy): minimum levered IRR, equity multiple, LP IRR after the waterfall, base and stressed DSCR, debt yield, maximum LTV and LTC, maximum equity at closing, and minimum downside-case IRR. The price is searched, never scaled: at every candidate price the engine is re-run on a scratch copy with that `purchasePrice`, so closing costs, loan sizing and fees, equity, debt service, cash flows, exit and the waterfall all move. A 33-price grid (25% to 150% of asking) finds where feasibility ends and bisection refines it to 0.001% of asking.

The sheet reports the maximum bid, its gap to asking, the binding constraint (the one the engine violates just above the answer), each constraint's own limit, and a verification that re-runs the engine just below (all constraints met) and just above (a constraint violated). It says so when no price is feasible, when the constraints do not bound the price, when a constraint is not price-sensitive (for example DSCR when DSCR sizes the loan), and refuses to call the answer a proven maximum when feasibility is not one unbroken range of prices. Three scenarios, each a fresh engine run with its downside case, are shown: asking, the maximum bid, and an optional user-entered offer. Stressed DSCR applies an NOI decline to the engine's DSCR income and amortising debt service (it does not re-run a floating index; Financing Compare does).

**Test:** `prebid_test.js` runs the engine directly just below and above every reported bid (IRR, multiple, equity cap, downside IRR, LP IRR), re-derives the individual limits with a separate bisection, and covers the infeasible, unbounded, no-constraint and not-an-acquisition cases and that solving never modifies the deal.

## Deal Remedies (`src/166-bk-remedies.js`, `remedies_test.js`)

The Credit & IC group's **Deal Remedies** sheet quantifies what it would take for a deal that fails the credit policy to pass it, and compares four interventions side by side with the deal as underwritten. Everything is the engine's and the credit gate's (`bkDealGate`, the same gate as the IC Memo and Credit Policy sheets) run on scratch copies of the deal. "Pass" is selectable: no HARD exception, or no exception at all.

1. **Resize debt** caps the engine's loan-to-value so the engine sizes the loan at or under a stated amount (default: the house debt capacity), and reports the additional equity, revised DSCR, debt yield, returns and multiple. 2. **Maximum price** is searched (coarse scan, then bisection to 0.05% of asking) by re-running the engine and the gate at each price, and reports the cut in dollars and per cent and what fails just above the answer. 3. **Rent required** raises every residential rent (rent roll in-place and market rents, or the average rent) until the gate passes. 4. **Occupancy required** lowers vacancy until the gate passes, or reports that even 100% occupancy does not. Where an intervention cannot pass, the best case tried is shown and the sheet says so. Required rent and occupancy are what the numbers require, not an assumption that they are achievable. The analysis runs on demand (each solve re-runs the gate dozens of times, about half a second each).

**Test:** `remedies_test.js` re-checks every answer by running the engine and the gate directly at the reported value and just beyond it: the resized loan and the equity and DSCR identities, the maximum price (passes at the answer, fails just above, no passing price between it and asking), the rent increase (passes at the answer, a hard exception remains just below), the unreachable occupancy case, a user debt ceiling, a deal that already passes, and that the deal is never modified.

## Waterfall verification (`src/169-bk-wfchecks.js`, `wfcheck_test.js`)

The Equity Waterfall sheet ends with a **Waterfall verification** panel that re-tests the engine's distributions against the deal's own hurdle terms (read only; no distribution changes). IRR basis, per hurdle: the hurdle account is replayed from the contributions and the amounts applied against it (accrued balance less the ending balance), and the IRR of those dated flows is compared with the hurdle rate. A hurdle that was reached must replay to its rate (held to 5 bps, because the IRR is dated actual days / 365 and the accounts accrue monthly); one not reached must replay below it with a balance still owed. Equity-multiple basis: the LP multiple against each hurdle multiple, in the same two cases. Both hurdle conventions are covered (the LP's own contributions and distributions, and the legacy deal-level accounts). Also checked: no tier or catch-up is paid while a tier below it is still owed, every dollar available is distributed once (tiers, partners and the engine's own distributions agree, contributions agree), and the promote equals the GP's distributions in excess of its pro-rata share (and is nil when Tier 1 is not cleared).

**Test:** `wfcheck_test.js` replays the accounts from the LP's receipts alone with its own IRR solver and compares; covers a deal that clears all three hurdles (replays 7.00% / 10.00% / 15.00% within 0.3 bps), one that clears none, one between hurdles, the equity-multiple basis and the legacy convention; and confirms that tampered waterfalls (extra cash to the LP, a tier paid while a lower one is owed, a wrong promote, distributions that do not add up) fail.

### Waterfall: partner breakdown, IRR-and-multiple hurdles, separate promotes, sponsor fee tie-out

- **Partner distribution breakdown** (Equity Waterfall sheet): each partner's distributions as return of capital, preferred return (the return to the first multiple on the equity-multiple basis), excess cash flow and promote, with contributions, profit, IRR and multiple per partner and in total. Tier-1 cash pays the accrued preference first, then capital (equity-multiple basis: capital first); tier-1 cash beyond capital and preference counts as hurdle return. The categories add to the engine's distributions (a check in the verification panel).
- **Hurdle basis 3, IRR and equity multiple** (new, additive; bases 1 and 2 run the original code unchanged and the golden results are unchanged): each tier carries an IRR hurdle and a multiple hurdle, and the setting **IRR and Multiple Hurdles Combine** says how they interact: *both must be met* (the LP must still receive the larger of the two shortfalls before the tier is satisfied) or *either is enough* (the smaller). Tested on the LP's own contributions and distributions. Equivalences checked in `wfcheck_test.js`: both-required with no multiple hurdles equals the IRR basis; either-is-enough with unreachable IRR hurdles equals the equity-multiple basis.
- **Separate promote for sale proceeds** (new, off by default): with `promoteSplit` on, each month's distribution is taken as operating cash first (the deal's tier splits) and sale / refinance proceeds second (`t2LPs`, `t3LPs`, `t4LPs`, blank = same as operating), through the same hurdle accounts. The sheet shows the promote earned on each part, and a check confirms they add to the promote. Works on every basis.
- **Sponsor fee tie-out**: acquisition (in the uses), asset management (in operating cash) and disposition (out of the sale proceeds) fees are each paid by the deal once and received by the sponsor once, at the same amount, so none is counted twice or missed.

### Fill rule (engine-wide)

Three fills, three jobs, never swapped. Defined once at the top of `src/162a-bk-design-unify.css` as `--bk-fill-head`, `--bk-fill-head2`, `--bk-fill-key` and `--bk-fill-sub`.

| Role | Colour | Used for | Never on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Header** | blue-grey (`--ae-head`, `--ae-head2`) | Names things: panel title bars, column-heading rows, section-divider rows | A row that carries a value |
| **Key** | gold (`--bk-key-fill`, rule `--bk-key-rule` on the left edge) | The answer: grand totals and headline metrics (IRR, multiple, DSCR, NOI, loan, price) | A heading, caption or divider |
| **Sub** | light neutral tint (`--bk-fill-sub`) | A subtotal inside a table: quieter than Key, different from Header | Headings |

Everything else takes no fill: cells are plain, calculated cells use the editor's read-only tint, inputs are boxed fields. Status chips keep their own pass / warn / fail colours.

For a new page: use `tr.grandtotal` (or `data-bk-emph="key"`) for the answer row, `tr.subtotal` for a subtotal, `tr.sec` / `tr.section` for a divider, and never set a background in the page itself. `fillrule_test.js` walks every sheet at 1280 px and 390 px and fails on a value row filled as a header or a heading filled as a key metric.

A full polish pass added: no sideways page overflow on any of the 77 sheets at 768, 1024 and 1280 px (and phone), dark mode spot-checked on Overview, Debt, Cash Flow Report and Sponsor CF, status chips never break inside a word, and `lease_fix_test` accepts the two-decimal percentage display.
